# OPC Offer and MVP

Build the smallest offer a founder can truthfully sell and personally deliver. Treat every unsupported benefit, price, timeline, or demand claim as a hypothesis.

## Audit the evidence first

Read `BUSINESS-SOT.md`, `CUSTOMER-EVIDENCE.md`, the latest validation verdict, and any inspectable interview or experiment references. Preserve the student's wording and corrections.

Before designing the offer, write an evidence ledger containing:

- the target buyer and triggering situation;
- repeated past behaviour and current alternatives;
- observable costs or consequences;
- real commitments, objections, constraints, and contradictions;
- the source reference for each claim;
- unknowns that could change scope, price, delivery, or the decision to proceed.

Do not count an AI role-play, survey intention, compliment, founder interpretation, invented screenshot, fabricated quote, or uncompleted payment as customer evidence. AI cannot simulate a customer, payment, deposit, purchase order, consent, approval, or authorization.

If there is no inspectable customer evidence, stop before packaging the idea as validated. The student may create an explicitly labelled `unvalidated offer hypothesis`, but the next action must collect real external evidence. Do not write claims such as “customers want”, “proven demand”, or “validated price”.

## Create `OFFER.md`

Write one primary offer. Add an alternative only when contradictory evidence makes a real comparison necessary. Include:

1. **Evidence status:** validated elements, hypotheses, unknowns, and source references.
2. **Buyer and trigger:** who can buy, the situation that makes action timely, and who can authorize or pay.
3. **Controllable outcome:** the specific change the founder will deliver, without promising revenue, rankings, funding, health, legal, tax, or other outcomes outside the founder's control.
4. **Deliverables:** tangible outputs, delivery format, checkpoints, and completion date or duration.
5. **Included scope:** explicit quantities, channels, revision limits, access, and support window where relevant.
6. **Not included:** excluded customers, features, integrations, channels, responsibilities, and follow-on work.
7. **Customer responsibilities:** inputs, access, decisions, approvals, attendance, and response deadlines required from the buyer.
8. **AI and human responsibility:** what AI may draft or automate, what the founder must inspect, and what requires customer or qualified-professional approval. Never conceal manual work behind a claim of full automation.
9. **Acceptance criteria:** observable checks that both parties can use to decide whether each deliverable is complete.
10. **Capacity and timeline:** the maximum concurrent customers, founder hours, dependencies, and bottlenecks. Use `unavailable` when a required number is unknown.
11. **Price hypothesis:** currency, amount or range, billing unit, payment timing, taxes if known, refund or cancellation assumption, and the evidence behind the hypothesis. Label missing evidence; do not infer willingness to pay from problem severity.
12. **Next action:** one transparent invitation to the cheapest external test.

Use customer language only when it is linked to a real source. Paraphrase or anonymize sensitive notes and keep unnecessary personal data out of the workspace.

## Define the deliverable MVP

Create `MVP-DELIVERY.md`. Choose the least complex form that can test the riskiest commercial or delivery assumption:

- manual or concierge service;
- paid diagnostic, workshop, or implementation sprint;
- template, report, dataset, or knowledge product;
- clickable or no-code prototype;
- software only when the test truly requires working software.

Do not force a SaaS build when a human-delivered version can test the same assumption. Record:

- the riskiest assumption being tested;
- the MVP type and why cheaper forms are insufficient;
- inputs, workflow, founder actions, AI actions, customer checkpoints, and final output;
- included and excluded scope copied consistently from `OFFER.md`;
- acceptance test for each deliverable;
- founder-time budget, cash ceiling, tools, dependencies, privacy or safety constraints, and stop conditions;
- failure handling and the manual fallback disclosed to the customer;
- the smallest build sequence needed before the first external test.

When regulated, safety-critical, financial, legal, tax, medical, or privacy decisions appear, flag the need for an appropriately qualified human. Do not present the Skill's output as professional advice or authorization.

## Choose the cheapest external test

Test the assumption before improving presentation. Write `OFFER-TEST.md` with:

1. the single assumption under test;
2. the exact target segment and recruiting source;
3. the offer version shown;
4. one observable action requested;
5. the success, revise, and stop signals defined before outreach;
6. the test cost, founder-time limit, end date, and owner;
7. the response log with dates and evidence references.

Choose the lowest-cost test that can produce the required evidence: a problem-to-offer review, a scoped proposal, a live walkthrough, a paid diagnostic, a deposit, a pre-order, or a bounded pilot. Asking “Would you buy this?” is not a payment test. Only a real action performed by an authorized person counts; log declines, silence, objections, refunds, and scope concerns as evidence too.

Do not send messages, create payment links, charge money, promise delivery, accept terms, or contact customers without the student's explicit approval. Drafting those artifacts does not mean the external action occurred.

## Reconcile the result

After real responses return:

- keep raw observations separate from interpretation;
- compare the result with the predeclared signals;
- revise the buyer, outcome, scope, delivery method, price hypothesis, or stop decision only where evidence supports it;
- record conflicting evidence instead of averaging it away;
- update `BUSINESS-SOT.md` and the founder state only after showing the proposed changes to the student.

End with one decision: `continue`, `revise`, or `stop`, its evidence and confidence, and the next cheapest unresolved test.

## Exit states

- `drafted`: `OFFER.md`, `MVP-DELIVERY.md`, and `OFFER-TEST.md` exist; unsupported claims are labelled; no external response is implied.
- `executed`: an authorized target customer saw the real offer or entered the real test, and the dated response is inspectable.
- `verified`: the course-required reviewer confirms the evidence trail, scope consistency, acceptance criteria, human-accountability boundary, and result-based decision.

Never advance a state because the copy sounds polished. State the actual status, missing external action, and owner of the next step.
