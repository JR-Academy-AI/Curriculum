# OPC First Dollar

## Start from a sellable claim

Read the founder's current customer evidence, offer, delivery scope, price, and exclusions. If these artifacts do not exist, list what is missing and keep the sprint `drafted`.

Define a **qualified prospect** with observable facts:

- belongs to the named customer segment;
- has experienced the problem or owns the relevant workflow;
- can influence the purchase or can introduce the decision-maker;
- has a plausible need within the offer's delivery window;
- is not a friend or founder paying only to help the student pass.

Do not count a contact merely because their name is available. Record why each prospect qualifies and the evidence source, while storing the minimum personal information necessary.

## Build the sales packet

Create or update these artifacts:

- `FIRST-DOLLAR-PLAN.md`: offer, price, scope, exclusions, delivery capacity, payment path, refund or cancellation terms, target, sprint window, and stop rule;
- `SALES-PIPELINE.md`: anonymized prospect, qualification evidence, source, stage, last real action, next action, owner, due date, outcome, and evidence reference;
- `OBJECTION-LOG.md`: exact objection, context, category, response used, result, and the product or message decision it suggests.
- `PAYMENT-EVIDENCE.md`: a redacted index of real transaction references, offer/buyer linkage, amount, currency, date, provider status, refunds or reversals, reviewer and verification decision. Keep payment credentials and unnecessary identity data out of this file.

The offer must state what will be delivered, by when, what the buyer must provide, and what is not guaranteed. Do not invent testimonials, urgency, savings, legal conclusions, capabilities, or outcomes outside the founder's control.

## Keep external actions human-controlled

AI may research user-authorized sources, prioritize prospects, draft personalized messages, prepare discovery questions, summarize returned notes, and draft a proposal. It must not:

- send, schedule, or post outreach without the student's explicit approval for the exact recipients and message;
- impersonate the student, continue a live conversation autonomously, or conceal that content was AI-assisted when disclosure is required;
- scrape prohibited data, bypass platform controls, ignore an opt-out, or reuse contact data for an unrelated purpose;
- create a fake reply, meeting, proposal acceptance, invoice status, receipt, transfer, or payment screenshot.

The student sends messages, conducts calls, approves commercial terms, and controls the payment account. Follow applicable privacy, electronic-message, consumer, payment-provider, and platform rules. If permission or legal basis is unclear, do not send; record the compliance question for a human to resolve.

## Run discovery before persuasion

Ask about the prospect's last real incident, current workaround, consequence, owner, urgency, purchasing process, budget source, alternatives, and what would block action. Separate the prospect's words from the founder's interpretation.

Advance the pipeline only on an observable event:

- `qualified`: qualification facts are recorded;
- `contacted`: the student supplies evidence that an approved message was actually sent;
- `replied`: a real reply is referenced;
- `discovery-complete`: a real conversation note exists;
- `proposal-sent`: the exact scoped proposal and send evidence exist;
- `payment-pending`: the buyer has accepted a real next step, but cleared payment is absent;
- `won`: payment evidence passes the verification gate;
- `lost`: the prospect declined, became ineligible, or the agreed follow-up window expired.

Do not infer `replied`, `accepted`, or `won` from silence, a draft, a calendar placeholder, an unpaid invoice, or a founder's recollection without an evidence reference.

## Handle objections without arguing

Classify objections as problem, priority, trust, scope, price, authority, timing, or process. Confirm what the prospect means before responding. Change a proposal only when the new scope, price, delivery impact, and reason are recorded. Never fabricate scarcity or pressure someone who has declined.

Review patterns after real conversations. One objection is a case; repeated independently sourced objections may justify revising the offer, segment, proof, or channel. Preserve counter-evidence rather than rewriting the record after a loss.

## Verify revenue

Accept only inspectable, redacted evidence from the payment provider or transaction record that can be tied to the actual offer and buyer. It must show the amount, currency, date, successful or cleared status, and enough transaction reference to distinguish it from other payments. Do not store card numbers, bank-account details, access tokens, or unnecessary customer identity data.

An unpaid invoice, test-mode payment, refunded or reversed transaction, founder self-payment, gift, loan, transfer from a friend with no genuine purchase, AI-generated artifact, or screenshot with no inspectable provenance does not pass.

Record the inspected result in `PAYMENT-EVIDENCE.md`. Record gross cash received separately from tax, fees, refunds, delivery cost, and recognized revenue. Do not make accounting or tax claims when the evidence does not support them.

## Decide and exit

Use only these states:

- `drafted`: materials exist, but required external actions or evidence are missing;
- `executed`: real outreach or sales conversations are evidenced, but the first-payment gate is not verified;
- `verified`: an authorized reviewer has inspected the qualifying payment evidence and its link to the delivered or deliverable offer.

Finish with the actual pipeline counts, strongest objections, contradictory evidence, offer changes, next action, and one of `continue`, `revise`, or `stop`. A sprint without a sale is still useful evidence; never convert it into a fictitious win. When the result is inconclusive, state what sample or event is missing rather than marking zero as proof of no demand.
