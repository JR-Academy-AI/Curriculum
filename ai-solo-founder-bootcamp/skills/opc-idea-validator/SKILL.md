# OPC Idea Validator

## Prepare interviews

Read `BUSINESS-SOT.md`. Draft 8-12 questions about the person's last real experience, current workaround, consequences, budget source, decision process, and prior attempts. Do not pitch the solution or ask whether they would buy it.

The student, not AI, conducts the interviews. Keep the artifact `drafted` and record the missing external action until real notes return. AI role-play may be used only to inspect whether questions are leading; its answers never enter customer evidence.

## Process evidence

Create `CUSTOMER-EVIDENCE.md` with one row per real conversation: anonymized segment, interview date, past behaviour, current alternative, observable cost, decision process, commitment, contradiction, and evidence reference. Separate quotes from interpretation and keep unnecessary personal data out of the workspace.

Score evidence, not enthusiasm. Commitments may include a second meeting, introduction, permission to inspect a redacted workflow, a pilot, deposit, or payment. A compliment, survey click, future intent, AI simulation, or a founder's interpretation is not a commitment.

When comparing candidate ideas, define must-have gates, weights, red flags, and evidence confidence before calculating totals. Run a sensitivity check with at least one plausible alternative weighting. Never invent customer facts to fill an empty matrix.

## Decide

Write a `Validation verdict` of `continue`, `revise`, or `stop`, with supporting evidence, disconfirming evidence, confidence, and the next cheapest external test. Do not force a positive conclusion or treat a five-person majority as population proof.

## Exit

Require five real conversations for the course gate. Mark the work `executed` after the conversations and evidence table exist. Mark it `verified` only when the evidence is inspectable, the written verdict includes contradictory evidence, and the required reviewer accepts it. If fewer than five conversations exist, keep the work `drafted`, state the actual count, and schedule the missing conversations.
